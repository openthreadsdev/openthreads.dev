If you sell physical products from the UK into the EU, the **General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR)** applies to you. It has been enforceable since December 2024, and by now, most EU member states are actively enforcing it.

This post explains what the regulation actually says, what it means in practice, and what you need to do about it.

---

## What is GPSR?

GPSR stands for the General Product Safety Regulation—formally, [Regulation (EU) 2023/988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj). It replaced the older General Product Safety Directive (GPSD) from 2001.

Unlike a directive, which each EU country interprets and implements separately, a regulation applies directly and uniformly across all EU and EEA member states. The rules are the same whether your customer is in France, Germany, or Ireland.

The regulation covers virtually all non-food consumer products—whether new, second-hand, repaired, or refurbished. If a product was designed for consumers and you sell it into the EU, GPSR likely applies.

---

## Why UK sellers specifically?

Post-Brexit, the UK sits outside the EU Single Market. That means UK-based businesses are now treated as non-EU economic operators under GPSR—the same as sellers based in the US, China, or anywhere else outside the EU.

This matters because several GPSR requirements assume there is a responsible party physically located within the EU. If your business is UK-based, you cannot fulfil that role yourself.

GPSR also applies to goods sold into Northern Ireland under the Windsor Framework, which means some UK sellers face compliance obligations even for domestic orders.

---

## What GPSR requires, in plain English

Here is what the regulation asks of you as a seller placing products on the EU market.

### 1. Appoint an EU-based responsible person

This is the most significant change for UK sellers. You must designate someone located in the EU who is accountable for product safety compliance and who can liaise with market surveillance authorities.

This person can be:

- A manufacturer based in the EU
- An authorised representative
- An importer
- A distributor
- A fulfilment service provider

If you sell B2B and your buyer is an EU-based importer, they may fulfil this role. But if you sell directly to EU consumers—which most Shopify merchants do—you need to appoint someone.

There are third-party services that act as EU responsible persons for a recurring fee.

### 2. Label your products correctly

Under Article 9, every product must include:

- A type, batch, or serial number—visible on the product itself, or on packaging if the product is too small
- The manufacturer's name, registered trade name or trademark
- A postal address and electronic contact for the manufacturer
- If the manufacturer is outside the EU, the name and contact details of the EU responsible person

Instructions and safety information must be provided in a language understood by consumers in the country where the product is sold.

[Digital labels](/blog/digital-product-passports-for-small-merchants) (such as QR codes) can supplement physical labels, but they cannot replace them.

### 3. Maintain technical documentation

Manufacturers and importers must keep technical documentation for each product for 10 years. This includes:

- A general product description
- A risk analysis covering foreseeable use and misuse
- Test reports, where applicable
- References to relevant EU standards
- Labelling and instruction information
- [Traceability records](/blog/traceability-without-blockchain) (supplier details, batch numbers)

You do not necessarily need laboratory testing for every product. But you do need to show you assessed the risks and can evidence your reasoning.

### 4. Meet distance selling requirements

Article 19 introduces specific rules for online sellers. Your product listing must display:

- An image of the product
- The manufacturer's name and contact information
- Product identification details (type, batch, or serial number)
- Any relevant warnings or safety information

This applies to your Shopify storefront. If you sell into the EU, these details need to be visible to the customer before purchase.

### 5. Report safety incidents

If you become aware of a serious product safety risk or accident, you must report it through the EU Safety Business Gateway within two working days. This applies regardless of where your business is based—if the product is on the EU market, the reporting obligation applies.

## What about online marketplaces?

Article 22 imposes obligations on online marketplace providers. If you sell through a platform like Amazon or Etsy alongside your own Shopify store, those platforms have their own GPSR compliance requirements—including registering with the EU Safety Gate and cooperating with authorities to remove unsafe products.

Amazon, for example, already requires GPSR-related fields in product listings. Shopify does not currently offer built-in GPSR compliance tooling, which means Shopify merchants need to manage this themselves or use third-party tools.

---

## What are the penalties?

The GPSR does not set a single EU-wide fine. Instead, Article 44 requires each member state to establish "effective, proportionate, and dissuasive" penalties. In practice, this means:

- Fines of up to EUR 100,000 in some member states for labelling and documentation failures
- Fines of up to EUR 2 million or 5% of annual turnover for placing dangerous products on the market
- Product recalls and market bans
- Customs seizure of non-compliant shipments entering the EU
- Listing removal on marketplaces

Several member states are still finalising their penalty frameworks. Germany, for instance, delayed its implementation after pushback on proposed fine levels. But enforcement is active and will continue to tighten throughout 2026.

---

## What about the UK's own product safety rules?

The UK passed the [Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/20), which received Royal Assent in July 2025. This is framework legislation—it gives the government powers to update product safety rules through secondary legislation, which is expected throughout 2026.

The Act allows the UK to either align with or diverge from EU standards. For now, UK domestic product safety rules remain largely based on the pre-Brexit framework, but this is expected to evolve.

If you sell in both the UK and EU markets, you will need to track both regulatory regimes going forward.

## A practical checklist for UK Shopify sellers

If you sell physical consumer products from the UK into the EU, here is what to prioritise:

- **Appoint an EU responsible person.** This is non-negotiable for direct-to-consumer sales. Budget for the recurring cost of a third-party representative if you do not have an EU-based partner.
- **Update your product labels.** Ensure every product includes manufacturer details, a batch or serial number, and the EU responsible person's contact information.
- **Prepare your [technical documentation](/blog/audit-ready-product-records).** You need a risk assessment and traceability records for each product. These do not need to be complex, but they need to exist and be accessible for 10 years.
- **Update your Shopify product listings.** Include manufacturer details, product identifiers, and safety information on each product page. This is a requirement under the distance selling rules.
- **Set up an incident reporting process.** Know how to report to the EU Safety Business Gateway if a product safety issue arises. The two-day window is tight.
- **Keep records structured.** [Spreadsheets work until they don't](/blog/why-compliance-fails-in-spreadsheets). If you are managing compliance data across more than a handful of products, a structured system reduces risk and saves time during audits.
